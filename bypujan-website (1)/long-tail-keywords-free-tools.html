How to Find Long-Tail Keywords Using Only Free Tools

By Pujan, November 6, 2026

Long-tail keywords are longer, more specific phrases, such as "how much does a restaurant website cost in Biratnagar" instead of "website cost". They usually show clearer intent, and you can find them without paid tools.

Free tools worth using

  • Google autocomplete: real suggestions as you type.
  • People Also Ask and related searches: questions people ask next.
  • Search Console: the queries your own site already appears for.
  • Google Trends: compare phrases and see seasonal patterns.
  • Keyword Planner: free with a Google Ads account, with rough volume ranges.

Method 1: Alphabet soup

Type your seed phrase, then add a space and one letter at a time (a, b, c and so on). Autocomplete shows suggestions for each letter. Write down the useful ones. It takes ten minutes and often reveals phrases you would never think of.

Method 2: Question words and modifiers

Put a question word before your seed: how, what, why, can, which. Then add modifiers after it:

TypeExamples of modifiers
Placein Nepal, Biratnagar, Kathmandu
Moneycost, price, cheap, packages
Audiencefor beginners, for students, for small business
Comparisonvs, best, alternative

Method 3: Find striking-distance queries

In Search Console, open the Performance report and look for queries where your page appears but sits a little below the first page, with a decent number of impressions. Improving the content and title for those queries is often easier than starting a new page.

Method 4: Read what Google shows

Search a long-tail phrase and read the top results. If several results are very short, thin, or outdated, you may be able to write something more complete. If the top results are strong guides from major sites, pick a more specific angle instead.

Turn the list into pages

  1. Group phrases that share one intent.
  2. Pick the clearest phrase as the page's main keyword.
  3. Use the others as headings and questions inside the same page.
  4. Link related pages to each other.

One warning about volume

Free tools often show very low or zero volume for specific phrases. That does not always mean nobody searches them. Specific phrases tend to be asked by people who know what they want, so judge them by fit with your offer as much as by the volume number.
