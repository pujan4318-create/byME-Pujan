How to Do Keyword Research for a Nepali Website: A Step-by-Step Method

By Pujan, November 6, 2026

Keyword research is finding the exact phrases your customers type into Google, then deciding which ones your website can realistically answer. Here is the six-step method I use, built for how people in Nepal search.

Step 1: Start with your customers' own words

Open your WhatsApp chats, call notes, and Facebook inbox. Write down the questions people ask before they buy. Your customers' words beat the words you assume they use. A restaurant owner, for example, would say "website for my restaurant", never "digital presence solution".

  • Aim for 20 to 30 phrases to begin with.
  • Include city names such as Biratnagar, Kathmandu, or Pokhara.
  • Include price questions. They usually signal someone ready to buy.

Step 2: Expand with Google's own suggestions

Type each phrase into Google and note the autocomplete suggestions. Then scroll to "People also ask" and the related searches at the bottom of the results. These come from real searches and cost nothing.

Step 3: Check demand, with caution

Google Keyword Planner is free with a Google Ads account. Set the location to Nepal. If you are not running ads, volumes may appear as broad ranges instead of exact numbers. Nepal is a smaller market, so low numbers are normal. Use them to compare phrases against each other, not as promises of traffic. Google Trends is useful for comparing two phrases over time.

Step 4: Read the results to understand intent

Search the phrase yourself and look at what ranks. If the top results are all service pages and you plan a blog post, you are mismatched.

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to publish
Service or product pagesPeople want to hire or buyA service page
Guides and articlesPeople want to learnA blog post
Map listings at the topPeople want a nearby businessA Google Business Profile plus a local page

Step 5: Use your own data in Search Console

The Performance report in Search Console shows the queries your site already appears for. Look for queries with many impressions but few clicks. Those pages usually need a better title or more complete content.

Step 6: Group keywords and give each page one job

Cluster similar phrases and give each page one main keyword. Two pages chasing the same phrase compete with each other, which is called keyword cannibalization.

Phrase (example)Best page

A simple way to prioritize

Score each phrase from 1 to 3 on three questions: is there demand, does it match what you sell, and can you realistically compete? Start with the highest totals. A phrase that matches your offer and has a few small local sites ranking is often a better start than a famous phrase dominated by big sites.

Nepal-specific notes

People may search in English, in Roman Nepali, or in Devanagari, so check all three. I cover that in the next article. City names usually signal local intent, so check whether Google shows Maps results. If it does, your Google Business Profile matters as much as your page.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing keywords by volume alone.
  • Ignoring search intent.
  • Trying to make one page rank for everything.
  • Never revisiting the list after publishing.

What I have left out on purpose

I have not listed search volumes. They change, and tool estimates for Nepal are rough. Run each step yourself and keep a spreadsheet with the phrase, intent, demand, fit, and the page you will use.
